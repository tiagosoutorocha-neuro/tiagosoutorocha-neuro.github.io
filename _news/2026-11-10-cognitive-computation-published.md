Our article **"Understanding Advances in Computational Cognitive Neuroscience Over the Past Ten Years: A Scoping Review"** was published online in **_Cognitive Computation_** (Springer Nature) on **November 10, 2026**, two months after its [acceptance]({{ '/news/cognitive-computation-acceptance/' | relative_url }}). It appears in **volume 18 as article 128** and is **open access** under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0) license, so anyone can read and download the full text for free.

#### What the review found

The review maps ten years of computational cognitive neuroscience (CCN), from 2015 to 2025. We followed the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology, reported it with PRISMA-ScR, searched PubMed, Embase and IEEE Xplore, and included **104 studies**.

- **Mostly theoretical research:** 63 of the 104 studies were theoretical, and very few integrated theory with application.
- **Main cognitive domains:** memory, learning, language and communication, and decision-making.
- **Main models:** recurrent neural networks and Bayesian models were the most widely adopted frameworks.
- **Tools:** Python overtook MATLAB as the most used language.
- **Open challenges:** neurobiological plausibility, model integration, limited external generalizability and the need for more translational research.

Beyond the reviewed studies, the article discusses transformers, foundation models, large language models and neuromorphic computing as possible next directions for the field.
